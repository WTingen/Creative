SHEET 01

Executive Summary

The foundation is solid — a real 20-year track record, a recognizable client roster (CBRE, Seefried, Apex Industrial, Beacon Communities), and a blog that's actually current. What's missing is proof and paths: the site asserts expertise but rarely shows the work behind it, and it offers exactly one way to convert — book a call — with nothing for a visitor who isn't ready for that yet.

01

Three repeated sections, one job each missing. The client logo wall and "Featured Work" appear on Home, About, and Work with overlapping names (Seefried, Apex, Hailey, Manor Lakes). Three pages doing the same job means none of them does its own job well.

02

No proof of outcomes. The hero promises "Speed ROI." Nothing on the site — not the Work page, not a single case study — shows a result. For a niche this data-literate (CRE, brokerage, development), that's the gap most likely costing qualified leads.

03

One conversion path. Every CTA on every page is "Book a call with Warren." That's the right ask for a ready buyer and the wrong ask for everyone still researching — which, for a five-figure branding engagement, is most first-time visitors.

SHEET 03

Positioning & Story

The homepage tag reads "Commercial · Development · Construction." The About page's own client list tells a wider story: alongside CBRE, Seefried, Apex Industrial, SVN, and Capital Development Partners sit Tanner Health System, West Georgia Urology, Tingen Law, and Your Haven Recovery — healthcare, legal, and treatment clients with no mention anywhere in the positioning. In context that's not a muddled brand — it's a business that took good work wherever it came from while the LLC got off the ground (2021–present), sitting on top of a much deeper, narrower CRE history underneath.

04

Pick a lane, don't erase the rest. Real estate, development, and construction are the strongest logo density and the sharpest story to tell — a niche buyer trusts a specialist over a generalist. Keep that as the primary positioning and add industry-specific pages for it (below). Fold the other verticals into a quiet "and beyond real estate" credibility line rather than diluting the hero.

05

The real differentiator is buried. In-house brand at CBRE from 2003–2009, then freelancing exclusively for CRE clients from 2009–2021, before forming the LLC in 2021. That's not "20+ years in marketing" — that's most of two decades inside the industry you're now positioning for, at one of the largest names in it. CBRE is also a current client logo on Home. "I started in brand at CBRE. Today, CBRE is a client" is a stronger opening line than anything currently on the site, and it belongs in the hero or the first line of About, not buried in a bio paragraph.

SHEET 05

Content & Messaging

Home

Replace the standalone tagline headline with a headline-plus-proof pairing: keep "Improve Deals, Speed ROI" if you like the line, but sit it next to one hard number in the hero itself — years in practice, brands built, or client count — instead of leaving that proof for the About page. Cut the logo wall and featured-work grid down to a tight highlight (3–4 logos, one flagship project) and send the rest of the traffic to Work, where it now has a real home.

Services > Brand Building

Mirror what already works on Monthly Marketing: turn the process into scoped offers. Something like New Brand Launch, Rebrand / Repositioning, and Property or Development Brand, each with a starting-at price or range and a typical timeline. A visitor comparing a $3,500/mo retainer against an unpriced project service will default to the retainer — not because it's the better fit, just because it's the only one they can evaluate without a call.

Work

Give the 3–4 strongest projects (Seefried, Apex Industrial, Manor Lakes are already the recurring names across the site — they're clearly the proudest work) a Challenge / Approach / Result treatment with one measurable outcome each, even a directional one ("cut lease-up time," "unified 6 sub-brands under one identity"). Keep the remaining projects as a lighter visual grid underneath.

About

Drop the repeated client list and featured work. Keep the founder bio as the credibility anchor, and add what's missing: how an engagement actually runs day to day, who a client works with, and whether there's a team beyond Warren for larger scopes.

Blog

The content itself is in good shape — keep the AI × CRE × branding angle, it's genuinely differentiated. Every post needs a closing block: a related-service link and an email or resource capture, so the traffic this content already earns doesn't leave empty-handed.

Contact

Add a phone number, email address, and general location near the form — not everyone wants to fill out a qualifier form before they can reach a human, and the absence reads as harder to reach than a 20-year firm should be. Add one line of reassurance after the form fields: what happens next and how fast.

SHEET 06

Offer Stack

Three documents you provided answer most of the pricing and lead-magnet gaps directly — the work exists, it just isn't on the site.

Built Brand Foundations → becomes the priced Brand Building page

This is exactly the scoped-tiers structure the audit called for. Three phases, each with a fixed price and a clear deliverable list:

PhaseTimelinePrice
I · Brand Master Plan2–4 weeks$15,000
II · Brand Build2–3 weeks$30,000*
III · Brand Capstone4 weeks$15,000

Put these three cards on the Services > Brand Building page in place of the current unpriced process description. Fixed numbers next to a fixed deliverable list is precisely what makes Monthly Marketing the best-converting page today — this gives Brand Building the same clarity.

Services & Rates → becomes the à la carte reference

The full rate card (Brand Strategy $5–25k, Logo Design $5–20k, Web Design $5–50k, Video $2–80k, $125/hr, etc.) is too wide-ranging to lead with — a visitor comparing a $2k service against an $80k one in the same list has no way to self-qualify. Use it as a secondary, downloadable reference for custom or single-item scopes ("just need a logo," "just need the website") rather than the primary pricing display. Built Brand Foundations stays the headline offer; this is the a-la-carte menu underneath it.

Real Brand Review → becomes the lead magnet

This already solves the "no low-commitment path" problem from the original audit — it just needs to move off a static, print-and-circle PDF and onto the site.

06

Rebuild it as an on-page interactive scorecard. Same six categories, same 1–5 questions, same score bands — but auto-tallied on submit instead of hand-added, gated behind an email address, and ending on the score-band copy that's already written ("Scored 69 or below? ... Did you score above 70? Congratulations!"). That copy already pivots straight into a call-to-action by score band — it's a ready-made nurture sequence, not just a PDF.

SHEET 08

Lead Generation

08

One CTA, one funnel stage — fixable with what already exists. "Book a call with Warren" is the right ask for someone ready to spend five figures on a rebrand. It's the wrong first ask for someone still comparing agencies. The Real Brand Review (see Offer Stack) is the low-commitment path this needed — it just has to move onto the site, gated behind an email address, feeding into the eventual call.

09

Testimonials are thin and undifferentiated. Three quotes, all reading similarly, one with what looks like a scraping typo on the name ("Tony Gimbroneone" — worth checking the source). Expand to 6–8 and tag them by vertical, so a construction-firm visitor sees a construction testimonial instead of only brokerage ones.

10

No proof numbers anywhere on the site. Six years at CBRE, twelve more freelancing exclusively for CRE clients, five years running the LLC — that's a real stat bar and a real timeline, not just a "20+ years" line. Right now that story only lives in one paragraph on About.

Punch List

Sequenced by effort against impact — close these out in order rather than all at once.

Phase 1 — Ship what already exists

1–2 weeks
  • Put Built Brand Foundations' three priced phases on the Brand Building page
  • Rewrite the About/Home opening around the CBRE → freelance → LLC timeline
  • Add meta descriptions and Open Graph tags to every page
  • Add phone, email, and location to the Contact page and footer
  • Rewrite the homepage title tag with vertical-specific keywords
  • Trim the duplicated client-logo / featured-work blocks on Home and About
  • Fact-check testimonial names before expanding them

Phase 2 — Rebuild the proof

4–8 weeks
  • Convert the Real Brand Review into an on-site interactive scorecard with email capture
  • Turn the CRE-aligned Work projects (Seefried, Apex, Capital Development Partners, dress gray, Horizon Living, SVN, ORIStruts) into Challenge / Approach / Result case studies
  • Pull the confirmed-usable pre-2021 CBRE/freelance work into the portfolio, narratively where assets can't be shown
  • Publish the Services & Rates sheet as the à la carte reference beneath Built Brand Foundations
  • Build the three Industries pages (CRE & Brokerage, Development, Construction)
  • Add a stat bar (years, brands, industries) to Home

Phase 3 — Build the engine

Ongoing
  • Add related-service CTAs and email capture to every blog post
  • Expand testimonials to 6–8, tagged by industry
  • Add ProfessionalService structured data sitewide
  • Stand up a Resources section as the home for future guides and checklists
